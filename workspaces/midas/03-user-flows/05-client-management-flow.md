## Handling a Client Request

### Scenario: Client wants to withdraw money

**What happens**

Sarah Chen emails you (or sends a message through the client portal): "Hi, I need to withdraw $50,000 for a home renovation. How does that work?"

**What you do**

You go to Sarah's account and click "Process Withdrawal."

**What you see**

> **Withdrawal Request for Sarah Chen**
>
> Requested amount: $50,000
> Current cash available: $58,300
> Minimum cash balance (her setting): $25,000
> Available for immediate withdrawal: $33,300
>
> **To withdraw the full $50,000:**
> Midas needs to sell $16,700 of investments to raise additional cash.
>
> Proposed plan:
>
> - Sell $16,700 of Bond ETF (has a $800 unrealized loss -- offsets gains)
> - Estimated tax impact: None (net loss)
>
> After withdrawal:
>
> - Account value: ~$1,190,000
> - Cash balance: ~$25,000 (at minimum)
> - All allocations remain within target ranges after rebalancing

## Your Regular Routine as an Advisor

Here is what a typical week looks like once you have clients:

### Daily (5 minutes)

- Glance at the advisor dashboard -- any alerts or issues?
- If something needs attention (a client alert, a system notification), handle it
- Most days: nothing to do

### Weekly (15 minutes)

- Review the weekly summary email Midas sends you
- Check which clients you have not contacted recently
- Send a brief check-in message to 2-3 clients (builds relationships)

### Monthly (30 minutes)

- Review monthly reports before they go out
- Add personal notes to any reports that warrant them (especially if someone underperformed)
- Review any pending client requests

### Quarterly (1-2 hours)

- Review quarterly reports for all clients
- Approve fee collection
- Contact clients who are due for annual risk profile reviews
- Review your own practice metrics (total AUM growth, revenue, client retention)

### Annually (half a day)

- Update your Form ADV (compliance requirement)
- Review all client risk profiles for those not updated during the year
- Prepare year-end tax summaries for clients
- Review your fee schedule (any changes for next year?)
- Back up all records and compliance documentation

## Scaling: What Changes as You Grow

| Clients | Your Time/Week | What Changes                                                                  |
| ------- | -------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1-10    | 1-2 hours      | You know everyone personally. Hands-on.                                       |
| 10-25   | 2-3 hours      | Batch operations become important. Templates help.                            |
| 25-50   | 3-5 hours      | You need the dashboard more. Personal notes become selective.                 |
| 50-100  | 5-10 hours     | Consider hiring a part-time assistant. Automate more communications.          |
| 100+    | 10+ hours      | This is now a full-time business. You may need staff or a compliance officer. |

Midas is designed so that the system scales, not your workload. The same automation that manages 5 clients manages 50. Your time increases because of relationships and compliance, not operations.
